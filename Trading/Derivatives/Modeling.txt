================================================================================
MARKET PHYSICS RESEARCH
Data Availability & Participant Modeling
================================================================================

Objective
---------
Determine what participant behavior can realistically be modeled based on
observable market data.

The objective is NOT to recreate the financial market perfectly.

The objective is to build scientifically defensible approximations grounded
in real exchange data.

================================================================================
MODELING PRINCIPLE
================================================================================

Never model what cannot be reasonably approximated.

If a participant's internal decision process cannot be observed,

do NOT invent it.

Instead,

    • Observe what is measurable.

    • Infer what is reasonable.

    • Validate against historical market data.

Every participant model should remain falsifiable.

================================================================================
LEVELS OF OBSERVABILITY
================================================================================

Level 1 — Directly Observable
-----------------------------

These become direct inputs to the model.

Examples

    • Price

    • OHLC

    • Volume

    • Individual Trades (Time & Sales)

    • Order Book

    • Best Bid / Ask

    • Funding Rate

    • Open Interest

    • Liquidation Data (where available)

Confidence

    ★★★★★

================================================================================
Level 2 — Inferable
================================================================================

These cannot be directly observed,

but may be inferred from market behavior.

Examples

    • Buying Pressure

    • Selling Pressure

    • Liquidity Consumption

    • Liquidity Replenishment

    • Trend Following

    • Mean Reversion

    • Momentum

    • Inventory Absorption

Confidence

    ★★★☆☆

These remain hypotheses until validated.

================================================================================
Level 3 — Unobservable
================================================================================

These should NOT be modeled directly.

Examples

    • Trader emotions

    • Institutional intentions

    • Proprietary HFT algorithms

    • Hidden orders

    • Future news

    • Internal hedge fund risk management

Confidence

    ★☆☆☆☆

Attempting to model these introduces speculation
instead of scientific reasoning.

Confidence

    ★★★★★

2. Derivatives Traders
----------------------

Objective

    • Profit from price movement.

Observable Data

    • Trades

    • Order Book

    • Open Interest

    • Funding Rate

    • Liquidations

Confidence

    ★★★★★

Can We Model?

    YES

Behavior can be approximated using observable
derivatives market data.

================================================================================

3. Market Makers
----------------

Objective

    • Provide liquidity.

    • Earn the bid-ask spread.

    • Manage inventory risk.

Direct Observation

    Impossible.

Indirect Observation

    • Spread

    • Quote updates

    • Order Book dynamics

    • Liquidity replenishment

Confidence

    ★★★★☆

Can We Model?

    YES

As a behavioral approximation.

================================================================================

4. Institutions
---------------

Objective

    • Execute very large positions.

    • Minimize market impact.

Direct Observation

    Impossible.

Indirect Observation

    • Persistent buying/selling

    • Large volume

    • VWAP-like execution

    • Block trade patterns

Confidence

    ★★★☆☆

Can We Model?

    Partially.

Only through observable execution patterns.

================================================================================

5. High Frequency Traders (HFT)
-------------------------------

Objective

    • React extremely quickly.

    • Exploit microstructure opportunities.

Direct Observation

    Impossible.

Indirect Observation

    • Rapid quote updates

    • Frequent order cancellations

    • Extremely short-lived orders

Confidence

    ★★☆☆☆

Can We Model?

    Only simplified behavioral classes.

Exact strategies are proprietary.

================================================================================
INSTITUTIONAL BEHAVIOR
================================================================================

We cannot observe institutional intentions.

Instead,

we infer behavior from execution patterns.

Example

Observed

    • Persistent buying

    • Increasing volume

    • Stable execution

Possible Interpretation

    A large participant may be executing
    a split order.

Notice

This remains a hypothesis,

not a confirmed fact.

================================================================================
THE ORDER BOOK
================================================================================

The Order Book is likely the most valuable dataset
for understanding market microstructure.

Reason

It allows observation of

    • Resting Liquidity

    • Incoming Orders

    • Liquidity Consumption

    • Liquidity Replenishment

    • Bid/Ask Dynamics

This provides insight into the mechanisms
that drive short-term price movement.

================================================================================
FOUNDATIONAL RESEARCH PHILOSOPHY
================================================================================

The objective is NOT

    "Build a perfect market simulator."

The objective IS

    "Build progressively more realistic behavioral models
     that explain observable market phenomena."

Markets are too complex to replicate perfectly.

However,

they can be approximated through careful observation,
reasonable assumptions, empirical validation,
and continuous refinement.

================================================================================
LONG-TERM VISION
================================================================================

Ultimately, the system should answer

    • What happened?

    • Which participants were likely involved?

    • What decisions could explain the observed order flow?

    • How did those interactions affect liquidity?

    • Why did price move the way it did?

The goal is not prediction alone.

The goal is mechanistic understanding,
from which probabilistic predictions can naturally emerge.
