=================================
Foreign Exchange Learning Roadmap
=================================

Part 1 — History of the Foreign Exchange Market

• If you want to understand why Forex exists, start here.

1. The Ascent of Money by Niall Ferguson

• Not a Forex book specifically, but one of the best histories of money.

• It explains:
  • gold
  • silver
  • paper money
  • central banking
  • exchange rates
  • international finance
  • Bretton Woods
  • floating currencies

• You'll understand why currencies need exchange rates in the first place.

2. Currency Trading and Intermarket Analysis by Ashraf Laïdi

• Excellent for understanding:
  • how currencies are priced
  • why exchange rates move
  • central banks
  • interest rates
  • inflation
  • capital flows

• This is closer to the real FX market.

3. The Battle of Bretton Woods by Benn Steil

• This explains the birth of the modern currency system.

• Topics include:
  • WWII
  • IMF
  • World Bank
  • Bretton Woods Agreement
  • US Dollar becoming reserve currency
  • collapse into floating exchange rates

• Without this event, modern Forex wouldn't exist.

=============================================
Part 2 — How the Forex Market Actually Works
=============================================

• These books explain who trades with whom.

4. Trading and Exchanges by Larry Harris

• This is one of the best books ever written on market microstructure.

• It explains:
  • bids
  • asks
  • liquidity
  • dealers
  • brokers
  • market makers
  • ECNs
  • order books
  • spreads
  • limit orders
  • stop orders

• This book answers questions like:
  • What actually happens when I click Buy?
  • Who is taking the opposite side?

5. Market Microstructure in Practice by Charles-Albert Lehalle

• Much more mathematical.

• Topics include:
  • order matching
  • liquidity
  • execution algorithms
  • institutional trading
  • price formation

Part 3 — How Trading Platforms Work
====================================

• This is probably what you're most curious about.

• Imagine clicking:

  BUY

• You might think:

  You
   ↓
  IQ Option
   ↓
  Market

• Reality depends on the platform.

• For example:

  Your Click
        │
        ▼
  Trading Platform
        │
        ▼
  Order Management System
        │
        ▼
  Matching Engine
        │
        ▼
  Liquidity Provider
        │
        ▼
  Interbank FX Market

• Some platforms are brokers routing trades to external liquidity providers, while others may internalize trades or act as the counterparty depending on the product offered and the broker's execution model.

• Understanding the platform's specific execution policy is important.

• Books on electronic trading explain this.

Algorithmic Trading by Ernest Chan

• Covers:
  • market data
  • execution
  • APIs
  • trading systems

Designing Stock Market Trading Systems by Bruce Vanstone

• Focuses on:
  • architecture
  • signals
  • execution

Part 4 — Candlestick Mechanics
===============================

• You specifically asked:

  "I want to understand the mechanics of candles."

• This is surprisingly simple.

• Suppose every trade arriving at an exchange looks like:

  09:00:00  1.10231
  09:00:01  1.10240
  09:00:02  1.10228
  09:00:04  1.10255
  ...

• These are individual trades (often called "ticks").

• A 1-minute candle is built by summarizing all trades within one minute.

• For example:

  09:00:00 → 09:00:59

  Open
  • First trade
    1.10231

  High
  • Largest price
    1.10260

  Low
  • Smallest price
    1.10220

  Close
  • Last trade
    1.10255

  Volume
  • Number (or total size) of trades in that interval, if volume data is available.

• A candle is simply a statistical summary of the underlying tick data.

==========================================
Part 5 — Recommended Learning Progression
==========================================

• Since you're building your own quantitative trading framework, study market microstructure before technical analysis.

• A progression could look like this:

  Money
      ↓
  Currencies
      ↓
  Foreign Exchange
      ↓
  Interbank Market
      ↓
  Liquidity Providers
      ↓
  Electronic Trading
      ↓
  Matching Engines
      ↓
  Tick Data
      ↓
  OHLC Aggregation
      ↓
  Candlesticks
      ↓
  Indicators

• Many traders start with candlestick patterns and indicators, but those are derived from the more fundamental processes above.

• Understanding how prices are formed, how orders are matched, and how raw trade data becomes candles provides a stronger foundation.

===============================
Key Distinction
===============================

• There are two separate concepts to understand:

  • How currencies became tradable
    • History of money
    • Bretton Woods
    • Central banking
    • Floating exchange rates
    • Foreign exchange markets

  • How a modern trading platform executes your order
    • Order Management System (OMS)
    • Matching Engine
    • Liquidity Providers
    • Brokers
    • Market Makers
    • ECNs
    • Execution policies
    • Order routing

• Understanding both provides a complete picture—from the historical evolution of currencies to the technical mechanics of modern electronic trading.

===============================
Recommended Starting Point
===============================

• Given your interest in first-principles thinking and building quantitative systems:

  • Start with:
    • Trading and Exchanges — Larry Harris

• Then continue with:
  • Tick data
  • OHLC aggregation
  • Candlestick construction
  • Execution algorithms
  • Market microstructure

• This progression builds understanding from the underlying mechanics of markets rather than starting with derived concepts such as indicators or candlestick patterns.
