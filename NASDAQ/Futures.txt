# Understanding Futures: What They Are and How They Differ from Forex and Stocks

====================================
Objective
====================================

Understand:

* What a futures contract is
* What it means for a futures contract to "track" an asset
* How futures differ from stocks and forex
* Why traders trade futures instead of the underlying index

====================================
Financial Markets Overview
====================================

The financial world consists of many different markets.


	Financial Markets
	│
	├── Stocks
	├── Bonds
	├── Forex
	├── Commodities
	├── Futures
	├── Options
	├── ETFs
	└── Crypto


Each market represents a different type of asset or financial instrument.

====================================
Stocks
====================================

A stock represents ownership in a company.

**Examples**

* Apple (AAPL)
* Microsoft (MSFT)
* NVIDIA (NVDA)

Buying one share of Apple means:

> You own a very small portion of Apple Inc.

The stock price depends on:

* Company performance
* Earnings
* News
* Economic conditions
* Investor expectations

====================================
Forex
====================================

Forex stands for **Foreign Exchange**.

Instead of buying companies, you trade currencies.

**Examples**

* EUR/USD
* GBP/USD
* USD/JPY

Buying EUR/USD means:

> You believe the Euro will become stronger relative to the US Dollar.

There is no company involved.

You are trading:

```text
Exchange Rate
```

====================================
The NASDAQ-100 Index
====================================

The NASDAQ-100 is:

* Not a company
* Not a stock
* Not something you can own

It is a mathematical calculation representing the combined performance of the 100 largest non-financial companies listed on the Nasdaq exchange.

**Examples of Companies**

* Apple
* Microsoft
* NVIDIA
* Amazon
* Meta
* ...

These companies collectively determine the value of the index.

**Example**

```text
NASDAQ-100 Index

29,000
```

The number **29,000** is simply the calculated value of the index.

Think of it like:

* Average temperature
* Average salary
* Average exam score

These are measurements—not assets you can buy.

====================================
Why Can't You Buy the Index?
====================================

The index is only a number.

You cannot purchase:

```text
29,000
```

Just like you cannot buy:

```text
Average Temperature
```

Since the index itself cannot be traded directly, financial markets created another instrument.

====================================
Futures Contracts
====================================

A futures contract is a tradable financial contract whose value is based on another asset.


	Underlying Asset

	      ↓

	NASDAQ-100 Index

	      ↓

	NQ Futures Contract


The futures contract allows traders to:

* Speculate on price movements
* Hedge against market risk

====================================
What Does "Tracking" Mean?
====================================

Tracking simply means:

> The price of the futures contract follows the price movement of the underlying asset.

**Example**


	NASDAQ Index

	29,000

	↓

	29,100


The NQ futures contract typically moves similarly:


	NQ Futures

	29,000

	↓

	29,100


The contract is said to **track** the index.

====================================
Analogy: A Shadow
====================================

Imagine:


	Person

	↓

	Shadow


When the person moves:


	Person →

	Shadow →


	Likewise:


	NASDAQ Index →

	NQ Futures →


The futures contract follows (or **tracks**) the movement of the underlying index.

====================================
Why Were Futures Originally Created?
====================================

Imagine a farmer harvesting wheat.

The farmer wants to know in advance what price will be received.

A buyer wants to know what price will be paid.

Both agree today:

```text
"I will sell

100 tons

for $500

in six months."
```

That agreement is a futures contract.

Originally, futures helped businesses reduce uncertainty.

Today, traders often buy and sell these contracts before they expire.

====================================
Modern Futures
====================================

Modern futures exist for many different assets.

**Examples**

* Oil Futures
* Gold Futures
* Corn Futures
* Bitcoin Futures
* NASDAQ Futures
* S&P 500 Futures

====================================
Nasdaq Futures (NQ)
====================================

NQ stands for:

```text
NASDAQ-100 E-mini Futures
```

NQ is **not**:

* A stock
* The Nasdaq-100 Index itself
* An ETF

It is a futures contract whose value follows the Nasdaq-100 Index.

====================================
Relationship Between Companies, Index, and Futures
====================================


	Apple

	Microsoft

	NVIDIA

		Amazon

				│
				▼

		NASDAQ-100 Index
		(Mathematical Calculation)

				│
				▼

		NQ Futures
		(Tradable Contract)

				│
				▼

		Trader


The relationship is:

* Companies determine the index.
* The futures contract tracks the index.
* The trader buys and sells the futures contract.

====================================
Forex vs Futures
====================================

### Forex


	EUR/USD

	↓

	Actual Currency Exchange Rate


You trade the exchange rate directly.

### Futures


	Companies

	↓

	Index

	↓

	Tradable Contract

	↓

	Trader


You trade the contract, not the underlying index.

====================================
Comparison
====================================

| Feature              | Stocks            | Forex                  | Futures                                |
|----------------------|-------------------|------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| What is traded?      | Company ownership | Currency exchange rate | Contract based on an underlying asset  |
| Example              | Apple             | EUR/USD                | NQ, ES, Gold Futures                   |
| Centralized exchange | Yes               | No (OTC market)        | Yes                                    |
| Underlying asset     | Company           | Currency pair          | Index, commodity, bond, currency, etc. |
| Can use leverage     | Sometimes         | Yes                    | Yes                                    |

====================================
Key Takeaways
====================================

* A stock is ownership in a company.
* Forex is the exchange rate between two currencies.
* An index is a mathematical measurement and cannot be bought directly.
* A futures contract is a tradable agreement whose value depends on an underlying asset.
* "Tracking" means the futures contract's price generally follows the movement of its underlying asset.
* NQ is a futures contract that tracks the Nasdaq-100 Index.
* Traders buy and sell the NQ contract rather than the index itself.
